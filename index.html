Lead Story · EU Policy

The Digital Nutrition Label: A Proposal to Make Algorithmic Services Tell You What They're Feeding You

The EU mandates ingredient lists on yogurt, emission stats on cars, and health warnings on cigarettes. But the algorithmic services shaping the information diet of 450 million Europeans — including children — have zero mandatory per-user behavioral disclosure. A new civic tech initiative wants to change that by proposing standardized transparency APIs: a "nutrition label" for your digital attention.

The concept, launched at digitalnutritionlabel.eu, positions itself as the logical next step in the EU's regulatory trajectory from GDPR through the DSA and AI Act to the forthcoming Digital Fairness Act. At its core is a deceptively simple demand: if platforms know how much ad revenue your attention generates, how many purchase decisions they've influenced, and how your preferences have shifted — tell you.

The New Feudalism: Are We Becoming Digital Serfs?

Goods and power were concentrated in the 17th–18th centuries, then decentralized, and are now reconcentrating around energy, compute, and materials. Varoufakis calls it techno-feudalism. The Hungarian zsellér (landless peasant) may be the best historical analogy for a future digitally dependent population. A critical examination of the reconcentration thesis — and where it falls short.

Sociology

The Nuclear Family as GDP Generator: A Theory of Structural Dependency

A provocative thesis: the nuclear family structure doesn't just correlate with high GDP — it generates the need for it. Without extended kin networks, every function (childcare, eldercare, emotional support, crisis response) must be purchased on the market. Connecting Illich, Chayanov, Polanyi, and Fei Xiaotong to argue that what we measure as prosperity is partly the cost of replacing what communities once provided for free.
