Beyond Starlink: Building Communication Resilience When Infrastructure Can't Be Trusted

A practical assessment of off-grid communication options: amateur radio, mesh networking, encrypted messaging. Hardware recommendations, Budapest-specific considerations, and a combined setup for under €1,500.

GK
Gergely K.
March 2026 · 9 min read

The Problem

The internet is centralized infrastructure. It can be shut down, throttled, or surveilled by any government that controls the physical layer — the cables, the ISPs, the DNS servers. This has happened in Myanmar, Iran, Sudan, Russia, Ethiopia, and many other countries. The question for anyone living in a democracy that may be backsliding is not whether it could happen, but what alternatives exist if it does.

Starlink is the obvious first answer, but it has limitations: it requires a visible terminal, it's controlled by a single company with its own political relationships, and it can be jammed or geo-blocked. A serious resilience strategy needs layers beyond satellite internet.

Layer 1: Amateur Radio — International Reach

Amateur (ham) radio is the only communication technology that is both independent of commercial infrastructure and capable of international reach. With an HF (high-frequency) radio and a wire antenna, you can communicate with people hundreds or thousands of kilometers away — bouncing signals off the ionosphere with no infrastructure in between.

The key digital modes for crisis communication: Winlink provides email over radio — you compose messages offline and they're transmitted through a network of volunteer gateways. JS8Call enables keyboard-to-keyboard chat with store-and-forward capability. VarAC offers a more modern chat interface over radio.

Hardware: The Xiegu X6100 (~€600) or ICOM IC-705 (~€1,200) are portable HF transceivers that can operate off battery power. Add a Digirig audio interface (~€50) to connect to a laptop, and a NVIS wire antenna for regional coverage reaching neighboring countries' gateways.

In Hungary, the licensing body is NMHH. The Budapest amateur radio club HA5KDR is a practical starting point.

Layer 2: Mesh Networking — City-Scale Coordination

Meshtastic turns cheap LoRa radio modules into a mesh network that passes messages between nodes automatically. On the EU 868 MHz ISM band, no license is required. Devices range from the Heltec V3 (~€18) to the LILYGO T-Deck (~€50) to the Spectre Pro for better range. Realistic urban range: 1–3 km per hop, but messages relay through intermediate nodes.

The practical requirement: you need a network of people, not just a device. Distribute Meshtastic nodes among trusted contacts — family, neighbors, civic groups — and establish them before a crisis. A mesh network you set up during an emergency is too late.

Layer 3: Phone-to-Phone — Encrypted Last Resort

Briar is an Android app that sends encrypted messages directly between phones via Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, with no internet required. Range is limited — essentially room or building scale — but it provides encrypted communication when absolutely everything else is down. Pre-install it on all devices in your trust network.

Combined Budget
< €1,500
HF radio + audio interface (~€650), 5× Meshtastic nodes (~€90), LiFePO4 battery + small solar panel (~€150), remaining for cables, antennas, and contingency. Briar is free.
